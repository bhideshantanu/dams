About the data

  • Source. National Inventory of Dams (U.S. Army Corps of Engineers), data updated . The inventory covers dams that meet federal size or hazard criteria; many small farm ponds are not included.
  • Counting. Associated structures (saddle dams and dikes listed under a main dam) are counted with their main dam, so each dam appears once. Totals include Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico and Guam; the map shows the lower 48.
  • Age. Based on the year completed, ignoring later modifications. The year is unknown for dams, which are left out of age figures.
  • Hazard potential follows federal guidelines. High: failure would probably cause loss of life. Significant: no probable loss of life, but economic, environmental or lifeline damage. Low: little loss beyond the owner's property. It describes consequences, not condition or likelihood of failure.
  • Condition, inspections and Emergency Action Plans are self-reported by owners and regulators, are often out of date or blank, and are shown here only as totals, never for individual dams.
  • Removals. American Rivers Dam Removal Database (CC BY 4.0), updated : removals American Rivers knows of in which the dam was taken out for its full height. Years, reasons and river miles are as reported by contributors; border dams count for each state.
  • Not an official product. An independent analysis of public data. For any specific dam, contact its owner or the state dam safety program.
